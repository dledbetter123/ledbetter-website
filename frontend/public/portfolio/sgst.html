Sparse Geometric Signal Transport

A geometric theory of the transformer: attention as parallel transport on a fiber bundle, with tokens as sparse Fourier constellations. Across 21 architecture versions benchmarked against GPT under matched data, batch and training configurations, David's spectral model V12.1 beat a parameter-matched GPT-Nano on Tiny Shakespeare. The program also surfaced CurvBias, a curvature-based positional encoding: a learned curvature term on the attention logits that drops into a standard transformer and beat GPT with RoPE in every run at every scale tested.

The question

Self-attention works extraordinarily well. SGST asks why it works, what geometric object it is computing, and whether a cheaper object computes the same thing. The wager is that the answer lives in differential geometry and signal processing: if a transformer block performs parallel transport of information (moving a vector along a path while keeping it consistent with a learned connection), then attention's all-pairs computation is one expensive way to realize that transport.

Attention is path-independent

The score between positions i and j depends only on the content at i and j, never on what lies between them. In The cat that the dog chased ran away, attention from ran to cat is computed as if the dog chased were absent.

Language is path-dependent

The meaning that accrues to a token depends on the whole route through the clause. Gauge theory describes this regime: carry a vector around a curved space and the rotation it picks up on return is its holonomy. The central conjecture is that the contextual change a token undergoes is a holonomy, and attention approximates it expensively and path-independently.

Attention's cost

Attention is O(T2) in sequence length and keeps an exact O(T) memory, the KV cache: at sequence length 256, on the order of 1.3×105 stored values per layer.

Six research lines arrive at content-dependent transport of state. The product of input-dependent transition matrices in a modern state-space model, AtAt−1⋯A1, is the Wilson line of a gauge connection: a path-ordered product of local, content-dependent, norm-preserving maps. SGST takes that equivalence as a design principle.

The architecture

The mature formulation models computation over a base manifold M (the context) with a fiber of representational features attached at each contextual coordinate q, split into K orthogonal sub-bundles. Transport between coordinates is gauge-covariant and runs in the spectral domain:

X̃q = Xp · exp(−Dω2 − iω ∫γ A)

  • The diffusive term exp(−Dω2) is a heat kernel: frequency-dependent damping that separates scales for free. Low modes travel far; high modes decay fast.
  • The advective term exp(−iω∫A) is a U(1) Wilson line: a content-dependent phase rotation that is the path's holonomy. This is the term attention cannot express.
  • One Parseval inner product, Re(h ∗ c̄), between an accumulated mode-state h and the current token c yields three geometric objects at once: the metric (magnitude overlap), the connection (relative phase) and a reading of curvature (how phase alignment varies across modes).
  • In the V12.5 constellation reframing, a token is a sparse constellation of active Fourier modes, and shared modes are the connections between tokens. No attention matrix is built. With 8 sub-bundles of 17 modes and 6 active in each, the support space holds about 5.7×1032 patterns. Stabilizing it took signed magnitudes, a pre-norm residual highway, and the observation that magnitude already encodes sparsity, so no discrete mask is needed.

The bar: V12.2 ablation

The spectral machinery was decorative

At 3K steps on Tiny Shakespeare, a plain state-space model plus MLP reached 2.267 BPC and the full geometric model 2.302, at 4.7× the compute. The spectral machinery took 79% of step time to make the model 0.035 BPC worse. Every later iteration had to clear this baseline before any geometric claim counted.

Within 1.6×, behind

V16, built on the idea that the inverse FFT is the mixing step, reached perplexity 275 against GPT-Nano's 173.

Caught and disqualified

An apparent 18× win was a leak

The variant V16b reached perplexity 36 at step 2,500, about 18× better than GPT at the same step. David traced it to non-causal leakage: a position FFT mixed every position, future tokens included, into each output. The result was disqualified.

Mid-project audit

The limit is memory

A scalar-fiber model carries about 102 state values per layer, a 16×8×8 matrix fiber about 103, and attention's KV cache about 105: a 128× memory gap. The audit's conclusion: no amount of clever routing compensates for 128× less memory.

What survived every ablation

  • Content-dependent state transitions.
  • A per-token nonlinearity. The FFN is the only nonlinear feature mixer in the stack.
  • The inverse-FFT round trip. Removing it NaNs by step 5,500; it is load-bearing for stability.
  • Associative matrix-fiber memory. Retrieval as q·S beats a scalar moving average.
  • Complex-valued state. Phase is how holonomy is stored.

CurvBias: a curvature-based positional encoding

The program's most promising applied result adds a learned, content-dependent curvature term to the attention logits:

scoreij = qi·kj / √d − ∫ij κ(t) dt

  • In the gauge hierarchy, absolute and relative position encodings are flat connections, and RoPE (rotary position embedding) is a flat U(1) connection with zero curvature. This encoding is the first member with nonzero, learned, content-dependent curvature.
  • It beat GPT with RoPE in every run at every scale tested.
  • It is a drop-in addition, compatible with FlashAttention and KV caching, with O(T2) scalar overhead, d× cheaper than attention itself. Measured overhead is 1.3–1.6×, against 5–6× for a holonomy-based alternative.
  • The repository frames these as early, encouraging results that need further study, especially at scale.

Where the program goes next

The gap to attention is a capacity gap. The forward program in the repository (V19 to V21) aims to close it without attention's quadratic cost:

  • Widen the fiber from scalar to matrix to operator, moving state capacity from about 103 toward 104 per layer (K = 8 to 32), with a real associative store per mode.
  • Combine U(K) unitary transport with the delta rule, the one untried cell in the six-way table.
  • Sparse, learned-band FFTs: O(s log s) transport on differentiable frequency masks, with blocks specializing to different bands.
  • Apply the curvature encoding to LLaMA- and Mistral-class models during continued pretraining, to test whether the geometric signal grows or fades with scale.

SGST has a sister project with a different approach: the Finsler Transformer, which replaces attention with geodesic flow on a learned Finsler manifold.
