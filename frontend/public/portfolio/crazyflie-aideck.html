Crazyflie AI-Deck

Autonomous nano-drones built on the Bitcraze Crazyflie 2.1 and its AI-deck, flying obstacle detection and wall following for target tracking and building mapping. David ran obstacle detection onboard, wrote the streamer that pulls in and decodes the camera's bits from the drone for a ground station running YOLO for target tracking, wrote RISC-V firmware for the deck's GAP8 processor, and moved the GAP8-to-STM32 link onto the deck's I2C lines when the stacked decks used up the serial ports.

What David built

  • Ran an obstacle-detection network onboard the drone, alongside a flight state machine driven by its sensors.
  • Wrote the camera streamer by hand: the AI-deck streams its camera frames over its ESP32 WiFi link, and David's code pulls in the raw bits over sockets and decodes them into NumPy image frames on a ground station, which runs YOLO object detection through OpenCV for target tracking. It was lightweight enough to run at a higher frame rate, annotated the live view with each object or obstacle the Crazyflie detected, and let teams work on any graphics platform. The viewer code is in CF2-Computervision on GitHub.
  • Cut swarm reaction time 40% with a C/C++ state machine.
  • Built odometry-based maps of the drones' flight trajectories.
  • Wrote RISC-V firmware for the GAP8 on GreenWaves' PMSIS SDK: dispatching work across its 8-core cluster, sending data over UART, and driving the status LED.
  • When the decks used up the Crazyflie's UART ports, carried the GAP8-to-STM32 link over the deck's I2C lines as a clocked, SPI-style protocol. The original firmware wasn't recovered; it is re-created and verified in simulation below, with the code in aideck-bitbang-link.
  • Presented the poster Energy-Efficient Onboard Autonomous Drone Navigation at URCAD 2022, UMBC.

Two detectors, in parallel

  • Onboard: obstacle detection. An obstacle-detection network ran on the drone itself, alongside a flight state machine David coded into it, driven by the drone's sensors. Together they let it detect obstacles and follow walls on its own.
  • On the ground: object detection. In parallel, the AI-deck streamed its camera frames over WiFi, and David's hand-written streamer pulled in the raw bits and decoded them into frames for a ground station running YOLO through OpenCV for target tracking. The live view was annotated with what the Crazyflie was seeing: each object or obstacle it detected.
  • What it flew for. Target tracking and building mapping. The onboard navigation is the subject of his URCAD 2022 poster, Energy-Efficient Onboard Autonomous Drone Navigation: Wall Following and Obstacle Detection.

Where the ports run out

The custom link, in simulation

A re-creation built from the wiring above, not the 2021–22 firmware. The GAP8 sends detection records (class, confidence, box) in 17-byte frames: a sync word, a length byte, the payload and a CRC-16. Simulated at the logic level, then decoded three separate ways.

Assumptions: 300 ns rise time on the pulled-up lines (the I2C Fast-mode limit) and the latency models above. Not modeled: real silicon, GAP8 timing jitter, a second I2C master talking mid-frame. Every rate up to the limit delivered 2,000 of 2,000 frames; the next rate up lost frames.

Which decks use which pins

From Bitcraze's deck table. Parentheses mark an optional or alternative use. Red cells collide with a pin the AI-deck needs; blue cells share the I2C bus, which I2C allows.

Deck pin usage from Bitcraze's table, parentheses meaning optional or alternative use. AI deck: RX1, TX1, SDA, SCL, IO1 boot, IO4 reset, TX2, RX2. Lighthouse: RX1, TX1, (SDA), (SCL). Loco: RX1 as IRQ, TX1 as RST, IO1 as CS, (IO2 IRQ), (IO3 RST), SCK, MOSI, MISO. Flow v2: SDA, SCL, (IO2 RST/MOT/IO), IO3 CS, (TX2 RST), (RX2 MOT), SCK, MOSI, MISO. Multi-ranger: SDA, SCL. Z-ranger v2: SDA, SCL, (IO2 VL53_IO). Micro SD card: (RX1 MISO), (TX1 SCK), (IO1 CS), (IO2 CS), (IO3 CS), IO4 CS/(MOSI), SCK, MOSI, MISO. Clashes with the AI-deck: Lighthouse and Loco on UART1; Loco on IO1; SD card on IO4 and its alternates on UART1. SDA/SCL users share the I2C bus, which I2C allows.
