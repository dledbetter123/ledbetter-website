Lingual-First Host Intrusion Detection

Representation, Controlled Evaluation, and the Limits of Language Modeling on System Provenance

Can a language model catch an attack from a host's audit log? David's master's thesis writes kernel provenance as a compact, readable language and measures what the representation gives against what learning adds. Frequency counts go surprisingly far, they stop where entities interact, and SENTINEL, a one-layer attention model over a node's named relations, picks up there.

The question

Audit data as a language

Host intrusion detection asks which recorded events on a host belong to an attack. Since Forrest et al. showed that normal programs emit a stable vocabulary of short system-call sequences, audit data has been treated as a language. The thesis asks what a compact symbolic representation of provenance (the record of which processes acted on which files, sockets and processes) preserves for detection, and what learning adds on top.

The data

DARPA Transparent Computing audit records of hosts attacked by a red team: CADETS (FreeBSD), TRACE and THEIA (Linux) from Engagement 3 in April 2018, and CADETS from Engagement 5 in May 2019. The main comparison is scored as analyst recall at a fixed alert budget: how many labelled attack nodes appear in the first N alerts an analyst would read.

1. What the representation supports

  • Each event becomes a clause of subject, operation and object. The object is replaced by a type code (for a file, its location and extension class), and repeated references to one object are linked by a pointer. The vocabulary stays small, free of host-specific names and readable, for example S:firefox#0 OP:WRITE O:FILE#5.
  • David rebuilt the serializer around coreference, resolving an object's identifier and its path to one entity. Scored with a training-free frequency table of benign clause counts, the rebuild moved CADETS from AUC 0.746 and oracle F1 0.284 to oracle F1 0.885 (0.843 on TRACE), under the ThreaTrace detector's released labels. The gain came from the representation: the detector here is a count table with no training.
  • These are best-case numbers. The benign reference is selected with the labels and the threshold (oracle F1) is chosen on the test labels. A threshold set on benign data alone gives 0.642 on CADETS.
  • On THEIA the table reaches only 0.203, because most attack entities there are not rare. A language model of benign entity biographies (each entity's history of clauses), fused with rarity around the rarest entities, lifts THEIA to 0.987, against 0.647 for the same model untrained. Same labels, with benign training data selected by them.

2. Evaluation choices change comparisons

  • David reproduced three published detectors from their released code: MAGIC, a graph autoencoder, within 0.0001 F1 of its paper; ORTHRUS and Velox close to their published averages.
  • The two published THEIA ground truths share only 25 of ORTHRUS's 118 attack nodes. Under ThreaTrace's labels, David's fused detector and MAGIC statistically tie (F1 0.992 and 0.987 on MAGIC's nodes). Under ORTHRUS's labels, MAGIC ranks 58 of its 66 attack nodes within 1,000 alerts and the fused detector none.
  • Metrics reorder detectors too. Ranking nodes by event count, with no training, scores an attack-detection precision of 0.342, inside the range of five ORTHRUS runs (0.260 to 0.561). The same event count places only 4 of 118 attack nodes in its first 93 alerts.

3. How far own-clause scores reach

On THEIA under ORTHRUS's 118 labels, ranking all 701,622 nodes at 93 alerts (the published operating point of ProvFusion, another detector), scores that read only an entity's own clauses, trained or training-free, place at most 17, even when the better of two training-free scores is chosen per node type with the test labels. The cause is the representation: it records what kind of object a process touched and drops which one, so it cannot express which entities interacted.

4. SENTINEL, a detector over named relations

SENTINEL changes the grammar to restore which entities interacted: its words are a node's (edge type, partner name) relations. It reads up to 32 of them, pools them with one attention layer, is trained on benign data to predict the edge type of a held-out relation, and scores a node by its most surprising relation seen fewer than twice in training.

  • Over five training runs a single model places 42.0 of 118 attack nodes in its first 93 alerts, and averaging the five models' scores places 62. David's reproductions place 4 to 7 for ORTHRUS and 19 for Velox.
  • In a one-hop variant, SENTINEL's context works as well with partner types as with names, but its predicted relations need names.
  • Most attack nodes on CADETS in Engagement 5 lack those names: only 19 of 124 ground-truth nodes carry the object names SENTINEL reads. The replication there was limited, so the result is specific to THEIA.

Rigor, controls and earlier studies

Self-audit

A label leak, retracted

An adversarial self-audit caught a label leak in an earlier calibration. The result was retracted and re-measured label-free.

Likelihood

Better loss, fewer attacks

A temporal model with lower held-out cross-entropy found 25.6 of 118 attack nodes against SENTINEL's 42.0, so held-out likelihood did not pick the detector. Reported as exploratory, since the scoring rule was chosen after one run.

Thresholds

Best case is labelled as best case

The 0.885 and 0.843 frequency-table scores use a label-selected benign reference and a threshold chosen on the test labels. Set from benign data alone, CADETS falls to 0.642.

Earlier study: OpTC

Supervised discrimination

On DARPA's OpTC data, within-host leave-one-agent-out AUC was 0.979 ± 0.016, and cross-tool transfer from Empire to Meterpreter reached 0.753 against an untrained 0.37.

Earlier study: LID-DS

Negative controls falsified lifts

System-call detectors V5 to V9 on LID-DS ran as pre-registered studies with negative controls. STIDE baselines (a table of short system-call sequences seen in benign runs) solve couchdb (0.997) and nginx (1.000), and the controls falsified several learned-model lifts.
